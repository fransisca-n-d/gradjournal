      Common questions

      What's the difference between rounds, course deadlines and rolling admissions?

      Rounds (Imperial, Glasgow, Edinburgh's Business School) split the year into windows, each with its own closing and decision date. Course deadlines (Oxford, Cambridge, UCL, King's) are fixed dates set per course. Rolling admissions (LSE, Manchester, Newcastle) have no real deadline: courses close once they're full, so applying early matters most there.

      When should I apply for September 2027?

      For the most competitive courses, between now and January 2027. Cambridge funding deadlines fall on 8 December 2026 for many courses, and Imperial advises applying within its first four rounds. Universities with a single final date in July or August still fill popular courses early, and visa applicants need extra time.

      How much do applications cost?

      Application fees range from nothing (Birmingham, Cardiff, Newcastle) to about £75 (Warwick). After an offer, international students usually pay a tuition deposit of £2,000–£4,500 before getting a CAS for the visa.

      What are CAS and ATAS?

      A CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) is the reference number your university issues once you've accepted an offer and paid any deposit; you need it to apply for a UK Student visa. ATAS is a security clearance some science and engineering subjects need for certain nationalities; apply only after you get an offer.

      How many courses can I apply to?

      There's no UK-wide limit for master's, but some universities set their own: UCL allows two taught courses per cycle, Imperial one form with up to two courses, and Glasgow one application per programme each year.
